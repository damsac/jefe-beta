Markup and margin calculator

Type your cost and a percent. Markup adds that percent on top of the cost. A target margin is your share of the price, so the same percent comes out to a higher bid. If every cost is blank or zero, the page does not show a bid, and it does not show $0. The numbers below are just to show the formula. They are not a price for a job.

Worked example

Suppose the cost you type is $600 of materials and $400 of labor. That is $1,000. Nothing here is a suggested price.

At 40% markup the bid is $1,400.00. Profit is $400.00, which is a 28.57% margin.

The same $1,000 at a 30% target margin bids $1,428.57. Profit is $428.57, exactly the bid minus the cost.

Formulas

  • Cost = materials + labor + other. A blank line counts as nothing. At least one line has to be above zero.
  • Markup price = cost × (1 + markup ÷ 100). Markup can't be negative.
  • Margin price = cost ÷ (1 − margin ÷ 100). Margin has to be under 100%.
  • The bid and the cost are rounded to the cent. Profit is those cents subtracted, so the numbers you see add up.

Questions

Why is 30% margin a higher bid than 30% markup?

Markup is on the cost. Margin is on the price. If you type a cost of $1,000, 30% markup bids $1,300. A 30% margin bids $1,428.57, because the profit has to be 30% of the price.

What if I leave the costs blank?

The page does not show a bid, and it does not show $0. Enter at least one cost above zero.

Can the margin be 100%?

It can't. At 100% the price would be infinite, so the calculator asks for a margin under 100%.
