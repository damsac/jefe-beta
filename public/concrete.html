Concrete calculator

Cubic yards and a bag count for a rectangular slab or pad. Thickness starts at 4 inches and waste at 10%. You can change both. The bag yield is the figure printed on most US bags, so check yours. Leave the price empty and you only get quantities.

Worked example

A slab 10 ft × 10 ft × 4 in, 10% waste, 80 lb bags.

Raw volume is 33.33 cubic feet (1.23 cubic yards). With waste the order is 1.36 cubic yards.

An 80 lb bag is about 0.60 cubic feet, so that mix is 62 bags. The count rounds up from 61.1. No price was entered, so there is no dollar amount.

Formulas

  • Cubic feet = length × width × thickness, after each side is converted to feet.
  • With waste = cubic feet × (1 + waste ÷ 100).
  • Cubic yards = cubic feet with waste ÷ 27.
  • Bags = cubic feet with waste ÷ the bag yield, rounded up.
  • Yields: 0.30 cu ft (40 lb), 0.45 (60 lb), 0.60 (80 lb).
  • If you type a price per cubic yard, cost uses the yards rounded to the hundredth. A blank or zero price shows no dollar amount.

Questions

Why does 10 by 10 by 4 inches come out to 62 bags?

The slab is 33.33 cubic feet. With 10% waste that is about 36.67 cubic feet. Divided by 0.60 cubic feet per 80 lb bag, that is 61.1 bags, which rounds up to 62.

What if I leave the price blank?

You still get yards and bags. No dollar amount is shown. Entering 0 does the same thing.

Can I turn waste off?

Yes. Waste can be anywhere from 0 to 100 percent. Outside that range the calculator stops instead of guessing.
