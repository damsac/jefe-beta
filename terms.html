4. Recording, consent, and your legal obligations

Jefe records audio while you walk. Recording laws vary by state, and following them is your responsibility.

  • Consent to record. Some states require every person in a conversation to agree before it may be recorded, and in some of those states recording without that agreement is a criminal offence rather than merely a civil wrong. These laws generally apply most strongly where people have a reasonable expectation of privacy — which ordinarily includes the inside of a home and often the rest of someone’s private property, where much of this work happens. If anyone else speaks during your walk — a client, a tenant, a member of your crew — it is your responsibility to know what your state requires and to obtain any consent it requires before you record. Simply telling people that you are recording is the easiest way to do this, and we suggest doing it everywhere regardless of what your state requires.
  • Licences and registrations. You are responsible for holding whatever licences, registrations, bonds, and insurance your trade and your jurisdiction require.
  • Contract requirements. Many states regulate home improvement contracts, requiring a written agreement, signatures, and specific terms once a job passes a dollar threshold. A document Jefe produces is a draft and is not guaranteed to satisfy those requirements. Making sure the agreement you actually enter into complies is your responsibility.
  • Other people’s information. A walk often captures gate codes, lockbox codes, addresses and client details. Handle that information the way its owner would expect — it isn’t yours, and it can open a door.
